SECTION 1: SIEM, OPEN-SOURCE SOC, AND WAZUH / OSSEC ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[1] Key: gonzalez2021siem
    File: pdfs/gonzalez2021siem.pdf
    Source: Gonzalez-Granadillo et al. (2021) - Security Information and Event
            Management (SIEM): Analysis, Trends, and Usage in Critical
            Infrastructures (Sensors)
    Relevant Information:
      - Defines the core functional pipeline of a SIEM: log collection,
        normalization, storage, correlation, alerting, and reporting.
      - Compares open-source and commercial SIEM solutions in terms of cost,
        extensibility, and deployment complexity.
    Used For:
      - Background / State of the Art: Establishes standard SIEM terminology and
        justifies selecting an open-source SIEM over enterprise commercial tools
        for a reproducible, zero-cost home-lab SOC.

[2] Key: chamkar2025wazuh
    File: pdfs/chamkar2025wazuh.pdf
    Source: Chamkar et al. (2025) - Improving Threat Detection in Wazuh Using
            Machine Learning Techniques (Journal of Cybersecurity and Privacy)
    Relevant Information:
      - Evaluates Wazuh as an open-source SIEM/HIDS platform and highlights a
        key operational drawback: high false-positive rates in rule-based
        detection.
      - Measures Wazuh end-to-end processing latency and event-per-second (EPS)
        throughput under load.
    Used For:
      - Problem Statement & Evaluation: Justifies tracking false-positive rates
        alongside detection coverage after adding custom rules, and provides
        evaluation ideas for measuring detection latency and resource overhead.

[3] Key: teixeira2019ossec
    File: pdfs/teixeira2019ossec.pdf
    Source: Teixeira et al. (2019) - OSSEC IDS Extension to Improve Log Analysis
            and Override False Positive or Negative Detections (JSAN)
    Relevant Information:
      - Explains the Agent-Manager internals of OSSEC (the foundation of Wazuh),
        including Logcollector, Analysisd (decoders/rules), and Execd (Active
        Response).
      - Analyzes the risk of automated IP blocking triggered by false positives,
        which can inadvertently lock out legitimate hosts.
    Used For:
      - System Architecture & Active Response Design: Documents how Wazuh agents
        and managers interact and justifies making the custom IP-blocking script
        time-limited and automatically reversible.

[4] Key: alboushy2026smartsiem
    File: pdfs/alboushy2026smartsiem.pdf
    Source: Alboushy et al. (2026) - Context-Aware Web Attack Detection in
            Open-Source SIEM Systems via MITRE ATT&CK-Enriched Behavioral
            Profiling (arXiv:2605.13337)
    Relevant Information:
      - Demonstrates Wazuh log normalization, alert severity levels (noting that
        level 0 rules are suppressed by default), and MITRE ATT&CK ID tagging.
      - Shows why single-event stateless rules struggle with multi-step attacks
        from the same source IP and evaluates detection against mixed normal and
        attack traffic.
    Used For:
      - Related Work & Custom Rule Design: Supports writing frequency-based and
        stateful correlation rules in Wazuh (grouping repeated events per source
        IP) and evaluating custom rules against normal background activity.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: MITRE ATT&CK, ADVERSARY EMULATION, AND GAP ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[5] Key: strom2020attack
    File: pdfs/strom2020attack.pdf
    Source: Strom et al. (2020) - MITRE ATT&CK: Design and Philosophy (MITRE)
    Relevant Information:
      - Foundational reference defining MITRE ATT&CK's taxonomy of tactics,
        techniques, and sub-techniques, including its Windows post-compromise
        focus.
      - Details core use cases: adversary emulation, behavioral analytics, and
        defensive gap assessment.
    Used For:
      - Theoretical Foundation / Methodology: Authoritative citation defining
        MITRE ATT&CK and justifying its use as the standard catalog for
        selecting simulated attacks.

[6] Key: strom2017finding
    File: pdfs/strom2017finding.pdf
    Source: Strom et al. (2017) - Finding Cyber Threats with ATT&CK-Based
            Analytics (MITRE MTR170202)
    Relevant Information:
      - Introduces threat-based behavioral detection, noting that attackers
        frequently abuse native Windows utilities ("living off the land").
      - Emphasizes validating detections by emulating adversary behaviors inside
        the actual monitored environment.
    Used For:
      - Core Methodology: Directly validates the thesis workflow of running
        simulated techniques, identifying baseline detection misses, engineering
        behavioral rules, and re-testing.

[7] Key: chamkar2024soc
    File: pdfs/chamkar2024soc.pdf
    Source: Chamkar et al. (2024) - Security Operations Centers: Use Case Best
            Practices, Coverage, and Gap Analysis Based on MITRE ATT&CK (JCP)
    Relevant Information:
      - Defines the SOC use case lifecycle (Rules, Logic, Action) and a Coverage
        Gap Model linking collected log sources to MITRE ATT&CK techniques.
      - Uses Atomic Red Team to test coverage gaps and guide rule tuning to
        minimize false alarms.
    Used For:
      - Core Methodology (Gap Analysis): Provides a peer-reviewed framework that
        closely mirrors the thesis workflow (baseline test, gap identification
        with Atomic Red Team, custom rule/action creation, before-and-after
        comparison).

[8] Key: lupinacci2026trace2attack
    File: pdfs/lupinacci2026trace2attack.pdf
    Source: Lupinacci et al. (2026) - A Graph-Based Approach for Mapping
            Kernel-Level Telemetry to MITRE ATT&CK (arXiv:2609.12841)
    Relevant Information:
      - Documents the structure of Atomic Red Team and Invoke-AtomicRedTeam
        (technique definitions, prerequisites, execution commands, and cleanup
        commands) for repeatable telemetry generation (e.g., T1055).
    Used For:
      - Experimental Setup & Reproducibility: Serves as an academic reference
        justifying Atomic Red Team as a controlled, repeatable adversary
        emulation tool rather than ad-hoc attack scripts.

SECTION 3: WINDOWS TELEMETRY (SYSMON), LOG MANAGEMENT, AND RULE ENGINEERING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[9] Key: kent2006nist80092
    File: pdfs/kent2006nist80092.pdf
    Source: Kent & Souppaya (2006) - Guide to Computer Security Log Management
            (NIST SP 800-92)
    Relevant Information:
      - Standardizes core log management concepts: generation, secure
        transmission, centralized storage, parsing, timestamp synchronization,
        and log volume management.
    Used For:
      - Background / Log Pipeline Architecture: Provides formal standards-based
        terminology and best practices for shipping Windows endpoint logs to the
        Wazuh server.

[10] Key: mavroeidis2018sysmon
     File: pdfs/mavroeidis2018sysmon.pdf
     Source: Mavroeidis & Josang (2018) - Data-Driven Threat Hunting Using
             Sysmon (ICCSP 2018)
     Relevant Information:
       - Explains why default Windows Event Logs lack sufficient visibility for
         detecting post-exploitation techniques.
       - Details how Microsoft Sysmon enriches endpoint telemetry with process
         creation, parent-child process trees, file hashes, and network/DNS
         events.
     Used For:
       - Telemetry Selection: Justifies deploying Sysmon alongside the Wazuh
         Windows agent to supply the granular events required by custom
         detection rules.

[11] Key: thomas2022hespids
     File: pdfs/thomas2022hespids.pdf
     Source: Thomas et al. (2022) - HESPIDS: A Hierarchical and Extensible
             System for Process Injection Detection Using Sysmon (HICSS 2022)
     Relevant Information:
       - Implements a Windows lab using a tailored Sysmon configuration and
         tests custom detection logic against Atomic Red Team process injection
         techniques (T1055).
       - Demonstrates how proper Sysmon event filtering prevents false positives
         across thousands of benign Windows background events.
     Used For:
       - Implementation & False-Positive Control: Supports the Windows + Sysmon
         + Atomic Red Team lab design and explains why tuning Sysmon rules is
         essential for keeping false alarms low.

[12] Key: shukla2025rulegenie
     File: pdfs/shukla2025rulegenie.pdf
     Source: Shukla et al. (2025) - RuleGenie: SIEM Detection Rule Set
             Optimization (arXiv:2505.06701)
     Relevant Information:
       - Examines how overly broad or redundant SIEM rules inflate false-positive
         rates.
       - Defines concrete evaluation criteria for rule quality, including
         conditional scoping, filtering precision, recall, and false-positive
         rate.
     Used For:
       - Detection Engineering & Evaluation Metrics: Supplies the formal metrics
         (detection rate/recall, precision, false-positive count) used to
         compare the baseline Wazuh ruleset against custom rules.

SECTION 4: INCIDENT RESPONSE, FALSE POSITIVES, AUTOMATION, AND TESTBEDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[13] Key: nelson2025nist80061r3
     File: pdfs/nelson2025nist80061r3.pdf
     Source: Nelson et al. (2025) - Incident Response Recommendations and
             Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management: A CSF 2.0
             Community Profile (NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3)
     Relevant Information:
       - Current NIST incident response guidance aligned with CSF 2.0 (Detect,
         Respond, Recover).
       - Covers distinguishing events from incidents, accounting for known false
         positives, executing containment actions, and iterative improvement.
     Used For:
       - Incident Response & Remediation: Anchors the automated containment
         scripts (temporary IP blocking) and iterative rule-tuning loop in the
         latest NIST incident response standard.

[14] Key: alahmadi2022falsepositives
     File: pdfs/alahmadi2022falsepositives.pdf
     Source: Alahmadi et al. (2022) - 99% False Positives: A Qualitative Study
             of SOC Analysts' Perspectives on Security Alarms (USENIX Security)
     Relevant Information:
       - Empirical study of practicing SOC analysts demonstrating how excessive
         false positives cause alert fatigue, missed real attacks, and loss of
         trust in SIEM alerts.
       - Highlights the importance of environmental context when validating
         alarms.
     Used For:
       - Motivation & Evaluation Discussion: Top-tier venue evidence proving why
         evaluating false alarms introduced by new custom rules is critical for
         a realistic SOC evaluation.

[15] Key: kremer2023icsecure
     File: pdfs/kremer2023icsecure.pdf
     Source: Kremer et al. (2023) - IC-SECURE: Intelligent System for Assisting
             Security Experts in Generating Playbooks for Automated Incident
             Response (arXiv:2311.03825)
     Relevant Information:
       - Formalizes automated incident response playbooks and SOAR workflows
         (referencing open-source tools like Shuffle) where SIEM alerts trigger
         actions such as firewall IP blocking, process termination, or file
         isolation.
     Used For:
       - Automated Response & Future Work: Places the custom Wazuh Active
         Response scripts in the broader SOAR/automated remediation context and
         supports discussing full SOAR integration (e.g., Shuffle) as future
         work.

[16] Key: ukwandu2020cyberranges
     File: pdfs/ukwandu2020cyberranges.pdf
     Source: Ukwandu et al. (2020) - A Review of Cyber-Ranges and Test-Beds:
             Current and Future Trends (Sensors)
     Relevant Information:
       - Surveys virtualized cyber ranges and security testbeds, classifying
         architectures, scenario execution methods, and deployment practices for
         safe attack simulation.
     Used For:
       - Lab Design & Reproducibility: Justifies constructing an isolated,
         virtualized home-lab SOC testbed so experiments remain safe,
         repeatable, and easily rebuilt by others to reproduce the thesis
         results.
